### F-1 (P1): Student route A inherits SmolVLM2's NC/academic-only data provenance; the matrix calls the training route "commercial-clean" without saying so

**Evidence**
- `student-training.md:47`: route A's base is `SmolVLM2-500M-Video-Instruct (7b375e1)`, described as "cheapest on device". It is the
  only student under 1 GB.
- `licensing-matrix.md:46`: "The commercial-clean training route stays BuildPlan's synthetic data".
- `licensing-matrix.md:18` and `:39-41`: SmolVLM2 "(and 500M)" provenance is "a counsel item before any shipping use".
- The 500M card at `7b375e1` lists the same 12 datasets as the 2.2B card. Both say "3.3M samples … from ten different
  datasets".
- A fine-tune does not remove the base's provenance. "Commercial-clean" is true of BuildPlan's *fine-tuning data*. It is
  not true of the *model a route-A student ships as*.
- 005J had already named the cleaner base of this family:
  - `stage-reports/artifacts/analyzer-005j/licensing-matrix.md:37`: "SmolVLM-500M-Instruct (v1, image-only, `@a7da5b9`)
    … The Cauldron + Docmatix only … **the cleaner fine-tuning base** of the family".
  - 005M neither uses it nor explains why not.

**Why it matters.** `TRAIN_BUILDPLAN_STUDENT` is one of the allowed recommendations. Route A is its smallest-pack
route. As written, a reader of `licensing-matrix.md` §3.5 together with `student-training.md` §2 would take route A to be
commercially clean. That is a documented licensing implication that the cited sources do not support.

**Fix**
1. In `licensing-matrix.md` §3.5 and in `student-training.md` §2, add a licence/provenance column. The text should be
   along these lines: "route A inherits SmolVLM2's mixture (≈ 77 % of the card's declared instruction mix comes from
   academic-only/NC sets, F-2); route B (Qwen3-VL-2B) inherits only 'undisclosed'; route C is a Gemma Model Derivative".
2. Name SmolVLM-500M-Instruct v1 (`@a7da5b9`, Cauldron + Docmatix) as the cleaner route-A' base, or say why it is
   excluded (it is image-only, and so is this task).
3. Carry the caveat into `recommendation.md`.

### F-2 (P2): The size of SmolVLM2's restricted data is understated ("part of its mixture")

**Evidence.**
- `licensing-matrix.md:11` says "part of its mixture is non-commercial or academic-only".
- The pinned 2.2B card (`482adb5`, "Granular dataset slices" table) gives these shares by source:

| source | share | restriction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LLaVA-OneVision-Data subsets | 54.6 % | card: "only … academic research and education" |
| LLaVA-Video-178K | 21.1 % | same restriction |
| Vript | 1.0 % | "ACADEMIC USE ONLY" |
| ShareGPT4Video | 0.8 % | `cc-by-nc-4.0` + OpenAI terms |

- In total that is **≈ 77.5 % from academic-only or NC sources**.
- Permissively licensed sources make up ≈ 16.7 % (M4-Instruct CC-BY-4.0, MAmmoTH Apache, Video-STaR Apache, VISTA MIT).
- 5.7 % ("other-video/combined") is unattributed.
- Further, 19.4 % of the mix is Magpie-Pro text generated by Llama-3-70B and Qwen2-72B: `magpie_pro_ft3_80b_*` on the
  card, which is `magpie_pro(l3_80b_*)` in LLaVA-OneVision-Data. `Magpie-Align/Magpie-Pro-300K-Filtered` is tagged
  `license:llama3`.
- Minor: LLaVA-Video-178K has no YAML licence *tag*. Its card body says "License: Apache License 2.0" plus the
  academic-only sentence, so "the same" is right in substance.

**Assessment.**
- The dataset facts are accurate, and the conclusion is reasonable rather than overstated: an NC-trained model is not
  itself NC-licensed; this is a counsel item; research use is fine.
- The quantity is understated: this is the majority of the mixture, not "part" of it.
- One more understatement: "For research and teacher use there is no issue" (`:41`). Teacher use that feeds a *shipped*
  student carries the same counsel question in attenuated form.

**Fix.**
- Replace "part of" with the measured share and cite the card table.
- Mention the Llama-3/Qwen2-generated text share.
- Change "teacher use" to "research use; teacher use for a shipped student is the same counsel item".

### F-8 (P2): The guard does not cover native Android code, `tools/`, jniLibs or VLM-runtime dependency names

**Evidence.**
- `productionSources()` (`research-isolation.test.ts:47-62`) covers Kotlin, Java and `.gradle.kts` under
  `apps/android/app/src/main`. It does not cover `apps/android/app/src/main/cpp/` (`CMakeLists.txt` and
  `node_bridge.cpp` are tracked). That is the realistic route for a llama.cpp/libmtmd JNI integration.
  - A CMake `add_subdirectory`/`FetchContent` of llama.cpp would not be scanned.
- Also not scanned:
  - `apps/*/tools/*.mjs|sh` (e.g. `fetch-nodejs-mobile.mjs`);
  - `jniLibs/*.so` names;
  - Android assets with `.onnx`, `.tflite` or `.bin`. The 005M asset filter has only `gguf|litertlm|safetensors|task`.
- The §42 manifest dependency check does not name VLM runtimes (`@huggingface/transformers`, `@mlc-ai/web-llm`,
  `com.google.mediapipe:tasks-genai`, `onnxruntime-genai`).
- None of this is exploited in 005M: `apps/` is byte-identical and nothing in the current cpp or tools names
  llama, ggml, gguf, mtmd or the models. But the guard is the forward protection for "no VLM in Android".
- On regex quality: `importsOf` plus the `HARNESS` backstop over comment-stripped code catch every ordinary
  import/require/dynamic-import form, including multi-line and `export … from`. Only deliberate obfuscation (string
  concatenation) evades them. The gaps are of scope, not of regex.

**Fix.**
- Add `apps/android/app/src/main/cpp/**` (CMakeLists, `*.c*`, `*.h`), `apps/*/tools/**` and `jniLibs` to the 005M
  `HARNESS` scan.
- Extend the asset filter to `onnx|tflite|bin`.
- Add a dependency-name check for VLM runtimes.

### F-10 (P2): The pixel heuristics miss nested numeric arrays

**Evidence.**
- At `research-isolation.test.ts:284`, `\[(\s*-?\d+(\.\d+)?\s*,){64,}` matches flat arrays only.
- `[[r,g,b],[r,g,b],…]` evades it.
- Base64 and hex runs are caught by the 256-character check, and the 2 MiB per-file cap bounds the damage. Current
  artifacts are clean (see "Checked and OK").

**Fix.** Also reject 64 or more consecutive small-integer tuples, e.g. `(\[\s*\d{1,3}\s*,\s*\d{1,3}\s*,\s*\d{1,3}\s*\]\s*,\s*){64,}`.
