### performance and robustness

- Complexity: wallStripsOf is O(4·B + R log R) per inferStructuralLayout call (B = sheet bands, R = runs). The resolver calls inferStructuralLayout at most bounded×4 (+ shut mouths) times (plan-resolution.ts:653), so the added cost is negligible next to decomposePlan. The per-mass `wallStrips.filter(backOn)` is O(M·4). openFacesOf is O(M·G·|why|) and isWallStripZone is O(Z·G) with Z ≤ 4. There is no quadratic bands × strips × masses loop, and the only allocations are small run arrays and four closures.
- Crash paths: mpp = 0 or no registration is unreachable, because inferStructuralLayout returns at layout.ts:1695 when the envelope or registration is missing, before wallStripsOf runs. wallPx cannot be 0 (bandThicknessQuantile falls back to 12 px and the bands' min thickness is ≥ 3). Empty bands give cover 0 and the strip is skipped. A zero-length strip cannot pass minCell, and the cover divisor is Math.max(1, s1 - s0). Band.bounds, thickness, length and axis are always present.
- The envelopeStrips refactor preserves behaviour: same four rectangles in the same order (MIN_Z, MAX_Z, MIN_X, MAX_X), same minCell, and the `>=`/`>=` filter is equivalent to the old `<`/`<` continue for finite inputs. zonesOutsideEnvelope keeps the same ids via zoneIdOf.
- Determinism: the new code iterates no Map, Set or object keys. `names` is only indexed. runs.sort is numeric, Array.prototype.sort is stable, and the interval-union sweep is independent of order for equal starts. open/facesWhy follow the fixed envelopeStrips order. Gap ids come from stableId over canonical JSON. This is consistent with order-invariance.json (band and chain permutations, translations).
- Id matching: slugify('outer-face-zone-max_z') = 'outer-face-zone-max-z' (21 chars, under the 48 cap), the same slug stableId builds, so isWallStripZone's prefix matches. openFacesOf's prefix slugify('outer-face-zone-') = 'outer-face-zone' still prefixes those ids. No other gap slug starts with 'outer-face-'. openFacesOf matches the exact hashed id inside mass.why. assignRoles only appends to why and never rewrites it, so the ids survive into reconstructV2.
- No cross-reading leakage: reconstructV2 replaces `layout` wholesale on challenge or resolution (reconstruct-v2.ts:303/323/364), and composeStructuralLayout only concatenates draft and roofing unresolved (structural.ts:134). A wall-strip gap from a losing reading cannot suppress the winning reading's real zone through the hash-blind prefix in isWallStripZone.
- Boundary stability (±0.5 px) on the known trigger: from glog forensics, the south band faces at 536/553 against strip edges 536/553(.5) give deltas of 0–0.5 px against a window of 5.95 px; the 326 px band over a ~720 px side gives cover ≈ 0.45 against 0.25; and thickness 17 is well above 0.7 × wallPx. The real 0.5 px knife-edge is upstream and pre-existing: the walledEnvelope snap of 553.5 → 536 (margin 0.5 px). Both of its outcomes are honest: no strip with the body on the outer face and MEASURED, or the named gap with DERIVED. Mirroring flipping the decision is documented in order-invariance.json.
- Plan selection is unaffected: scoreReading (plan-resolution.ts) does not read mass basis or unresolved gaps, so lowering MEASURED → DERIVED cannot change which reading wins. Only legacy v1 reconstruct.ts:335 consumes basis (HARD → SOFT), and the service path (analysis-service run.ts) uses reconstructV2 only.
- v2 consistency: the MASS feature (reconstruct-v2.ts:408-410), MassV2.outerFacesOpen and the emit.ts ring status all derive from the same openFacesOf list, so the feature and the ring cannot disagree. The zone stays in footprintRegions, so v2/frame.ts (world frame) is unchanged, and G2 is the only ZONE consumer that skips it.

## Full findings (scenario and suggested fix, verbatim)

### METRIC-1 (P2) — backOn and the v2 zone skip apply to the whole side even though the rule fires on 25% wall cover, so a body cut correctly on its outer face can be labelled 'cut on a wall's inner face'

Scenario: wallStripsOf decides per side and needs only 25% of the side covered by wall-thick runs (WALL_STRIP_BOUNDS.cover). backOn (layout.ts:2251-2255) then tests a body's overlap against the whole strip [s0,s1], not against the covered runs. Example: a house and a garage sit side by side on the front side. The house's front wall is cut on its inner face (its outer-face line was thinned away, as on glogownikach), and its wall ink covers 60% of the strip. The garage front is set back exactly one wall, so its correct outer face lies on the same grid line (envelope.y1) with no ink in front of it. The garage also passes backOn. Its depthM goes MEASURED→DERIVED, its MASS becomes SOURCE_DERIVED, and its ring evidence says 'body cut on a wall's inner face; unresolved: the outer face of the building's front wall'.
